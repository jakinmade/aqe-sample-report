This is what a charity board receives after the Trustee Check: a short, plain-English view of whether trustees can show they are overseeing the charity's use of AI. It asks 20 questions across six trustee duties, drawn from the Charity Governance Code 2025, Charity Commission guidance (CC3, CC26, CC20), ICO guidance on AI and data protection, and the Charity Excellence AI Governance and Ethics Framework. The example is the same fictional wildlife charity used in the AQE sample and the CLEARANCE sample: staff have put sensible controls around its AI fundraising model, but the board has not yet taken ownership of AI.

Oversight required

9 of 38 points (24%). Several trustee duties show real gaps: no trustee is written down as owning AI, AI is not on the risk register, the board has not discussed AI risk, and nothing is scheduled for review. Data protection is partly in place, including signed agreements with the CRM supplier.

Reference TC-890A28A3 · Trustee Check rules 2026-10

The six trustee duties

Each answer scores 2 if evidenced, 1 if informal and 0 if missing or not known. A duty is strong at 75% or above, and developing from 40%. Only data protection reaches developing.

What the board is asked to do first

ActionGrounded in
Write down which trustee, or small group, is in charge of AI.Charity Governance Code 2025
Formally discuss AI risks at board level, record the outcome, and add AI as its own item on the risk register.Charity Commission CC26 (Charities and risk management)
Tell donors and beneficiaries when AI is involved in something that affects them.Charity Excellence AI Governance and Ethics Framework
Set a confirmed review date for the AI policy and risk assessment.Charity Governance Code 2025

Anyone can check the result

The Trustee Check uses fixed, version-controlled rules. There is no AI model and no assessor judgement in the result, so the same answers always give the same verdict and the same reference.

Every question in the report shows the source it is grounded in, so a trustee can check it against the guidance rather than take the report's word for it.
